# Subject: Quantum Physics (B.Tech CSE Core)

## Topic 1: Quantum Mechanics and Wave-Particle Duality
Quantum Mechanics is a fundamental branch of physics that explains the behaviour of matter and energy at the atomic and subatomic levels. Unlike classical physics, which describes objects using definite positions and velocities, quantum mechanics uses mathematical wave functions to describe the probability of finding a particle in a particular state. Wave-Particle Duality is a fundamental principle stating that microscopic particles, such as electrons and photons, exhibit both particle-like and wave-like properties depending on the type of experiment performed. The famous Double-Slit Experiment demonstrates this concept, where particles create an interference pattern similar to waves when not observed individually, but behave like localized particles when measured.

## Topic 2: Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle
Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle states that it is fundamentally impossible to determine both the exact position and exact momentum of a microscopic particle simultaneously. The more accurately the position of a particle is measured, the greater the uncertainty in its momentum, and vice versa. This is not caused by limitations in measuring instruments but is an inherent property of quantum systems. The mathematical expression is Δx × Δp ≥ ℏ/2, where Δx represents uncertainty in position, Δp represents uncertainty in momentum, and ℏ is the reduced Planck constant. This principle plays an important role in understanding atomic structure and the behaviour of electrons.

## Topic 3: Quantum Superposition and Quantum Entanglement
Quantum Superposition is a principle stating that a quantum system can exist in a combination of multiple possible states until a measurement is performed. For example, an electron can exist in a combination of different energy or spin states before measurement. When an observation takes place, the system produces a definite measurement outcome. Quantum Entanglement is a phenomenon in which two or more particles become connected through a shared quantum state, such that measuring one particle is correlated with the measurement outcome of another, even when they are separated by large distances. These principles are important foundations of modern technologies such as quantum computing and quantum communication.

## Topic 4: Schrödinger's Wave Equation and Quantum Tunneling
Schrödinger's Wave Equation is a fundamental mathematical equation of quantum mechanics used to describe how the quantum state of a particle changes with time and space. Its wave function, represented by ψ (psi), contains information about the probability distribution of a particle's position. The probability density is given by |ψ|². The time-independent Schrödinger equation is commonly written as Hψ = Eψ, where H represents the Hamiltonian operator and E represents the energy of the system. Quantum Tunneling is a phenomenon in which a particle passes through an energy barrier even when its energy is lower than the barrier height. This behaviour is impossible in classical mechanics but is observed in quantum systems and is used in technologies such as tunnel diodes and scanning tunneling microscopes.
